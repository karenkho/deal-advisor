# Glossary

## API gateway
A managed entry point in front of back-end APIs that handles routing, authentication, rate limiting and logging. In a TOR it usually implies integration work and an infrastructure assumption.

## Audit trail
A chronological, tamper-resistant record of who did what and when in the system (creates, updates, deletes, approvals, logins). Usually expected even when not explicitly requested.

## Business continuity plan (BCP)
The organization's plan for keeping critical services running during disruption. A TOR mentioning BCP usually expects DR documentation and a drill from the vendor.

## CI/CD
Continuous Integration / Continuous Delivery: automated build, test and deployment pipeline. Part of the Foundation epic.

## Cut-over
The planned switch from the legacy process or system to the new system, including final data migration, freeze period and go/no-go decision.

## Data Privacy Act (DPA)
The Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), which governs processing of personal information. TOR references usually imply consent capture, privacy notices, access controls, and breach procedures.

## Data Protection Officer (DPO)
The person designated by the organization to oversee data privacy compliance. Usually the approver for data subject requests.

## Definition of Done (DoD)
The checklist a backlog item must satisfy to be considered complete: code reviewed, tests passed, acceptance criteria met, documentation updated.

## Disaster recovery (DR)
The capability to restore a system after a major failure, usually stated with RPO and RTO targets and a DR site or region.

## e-Invoicing
Electronic issuance and transmission of invoices to the tax authority's system in a prescribed format. Implies a large integration with accreditation steps.

## Epic
A large body of work grouping related features, often aligned to a business capability (e.g., "Applications", "Payments").

## Go-live
The date the system is used in production by real users.

## Hypercare
A period of heightened vendor support immediately after go-live (commonly 30 days) to fix defects and stabilize operations.

## Interoperability
The ability to exchange data with other systems using agreed standards. In a TOR it often signals unnamed integrations that must be clarified.

## Know Your Customer (KYC)
Identity verification of customers, usually with ID documents, selfie/liveness checks, and screening lists. Common in financial-service TORs.

## LGU
Local Government Unit (province, city, municipality, barangay) in the Philippines. LGU TORs commonly involve permits, citizen requests, and fee payments.

## Master data
Core reference entities shared across the system (customers, products, offices, employees). Needs maintenance screens and often migration.

## MFA
Multi-factor authentication: a login requiring two or more factors such as password plus one-time code.

## Minimum viable product (MVP)
The smallest release that delivers usable value to real users, used to validate the solution before further phases.

## Non-functional requirement (NFR)
A quality attribute of the system (performance, security, availability, usability) rather than a function. Generic NFRs become assumptions; measurable ones become nfr features.

## OTP
One-time password/code sent via SMS or email or generated by an authenticator app, used for MFA or verification.

## OWASP Top 10
A widely used list of the most critical web application security risks. A common basis for secure-coding assumptions and security testing.

## Penetration test (VAPT)
Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing, often done by a third party before go-live. Remediation effort should be assumed or sized.

## PII
Personally Identifiable Information: data that identifies a person (name, address, mobile number, email, government ID). Must be protected and masked in logs.

## Product owner
The client representative who prioritizes the backlog and accepts features.

## RBAC
Role-Based Access Control: permissions assigned to roles, and roles assigned to users.

## Reconciliation
Comparing two sets of records (e.g., payments in the system vs. gateway settlement report, source vs. migrated counts) to find mismatches.

## RPO
Recovery Point Objective: maximum acceptable data loss measured in time (e.g., 1 hour).

## RTO
Recovery Time Objective: maximum acceptable time to restore service after a failure (e.g., 4 hours).

## Sandbox
A test environment provided by an external party (payment gateway, government agency) to develop and test an integration without real transactions.

## SLA
Service Level Agreement: a committed service target, such as response/resolution times for support or processing time limits in a workflow.

## SSO
Single Sign-On: users log in once with an enterprise identity provider (e.g., via SAML or OpenID Connect) and access several applications.

## Terms of Reference (TOR)
The client document describing the scope, requirements, deliverables, timeline and conditions for a procurement or project.

## UAT
User Acceptance Testing: testing by client users to confirm the system meets business needs before go-live.

## Webhook
An HTTP callback sent by an external system (e.g., payment gateway) to notify the application of an event such as a successful payment.

## WCAG 2.1 AA
Web Content Accessibility Guidelines level AA, a common accessibility conformance target for public-facing government and enterprise sites.

## Work breakdown structure (WBS)
A hierarchical decomposition of project scope into deliverables and work packages, often requested in TOR proposals.
